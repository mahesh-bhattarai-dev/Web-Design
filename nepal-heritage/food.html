01 / Everyday comfort

Everyday Nepali Meals

Meals that nourish and comfort, with recipes and ingredients that vary from household to household.

Dal Bhat & Thali

Dal bhat brings together rice and lentil soup, often served with vegetables, achar, greens or other sides. A thali-style meal offers a combination of dishes on one plate, with the exact selection changing by region and family.

Dhido & Local Staples

Dhido, made by stirring flour into hot water, is a traditional staple in several communities. It may be served with greens, lentils, pickles or other accompaniments. Foods and grains differ across Nepal's varied landscapes.

02 / Street-side favorites

Street Food & Snacks

Spicy, savory, crunchy, and perfect for a snack break with friends or a walk through a busy market.

Momo

Steamed or fried dumplings filled with vegetables or other fillings, commonly served with a spicy or tangy dipping sauce. Momos are popular in restaurants, homes and street-food spots.

Samosa, Pani Puri & Chatpate

Samosas bring a crisp shell and savory filling; pani puri pairs crisp hollow puris with tangy, spiced water and fillings; chatpate mixes crunchy snacks with spices, vegetables and a punchy sour flavor. Recipes vary from stall to stall.

03 / Celebrations & sweet moments

Sweets & Festival Foods

Special foods connect festivals and family gatherings with traditions passed from one generation to the next.

Sel Roti

A ring-shaped rice-flour bread with a gently crisp outside and soft centre. Sel roti is often associated with Dashain and Tihar and is shared at family gatherings and celebrations.

Yomari & Festive Treats

Yomari is a steamed rice-flour dumpling traditionally associated with the Newar festival of Yomari Punhi. Its sweet filling commonly includes ingredients such as chaku or sesame, and the shape and recipe can vary.

04 / Slow down & sip

Chiya & Everyday Moments

A warm cup of tea can accompany a morning routine, a break at work, a visit with a neighbor, or an unhurried afternoon.

Chiya

Nepali milk tea is often simmered with tea, milk, sugar and sometimes spices. People make it in different ways at home, at tea shops and when welcoming visitors.

A Pause to Connect

Offering tea or a snack is one simple way people welcome guests and make time for conversation. The ritual can be just as memorable as the drink itself.

A place at the table

Food is a way of welcoming people.

Sharing a meal, offering a snack, or pouring a cup of tea can be a simple expression of care. Nepal's food traditions are diverse, and every region, community, and family adds its own flavor to the story.
