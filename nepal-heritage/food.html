01 / Everyday comfort

Everyday Nepali Meals

Meals that nourish and comfort, with recipes and ingredients that vary from household to household.

01 / Everyday staple

Dal Bhat

A familiar everyday meal of rice and lentil soup, often served with vegetables, achar, and other sides.

Ingredients and preparation differ by region and home. Dal bhat may be a simple daily meal or a fuller spread shared with family and guests.

RiceLentilsAchar

02 / A variety on one plate

Nepali Thali

A plate or platter bringing together a selection of staples and accompaniments, offering different tastes and textures in one meal.

A thali can include rice or another staple, lentils, vegetables, pickles, and regional specialties. The exact combination changes from household to household.

03 / Traditional staples

Dhido, Gundruk & Achar

Dhido is a traditional staple made by cooking flour with water. Gundruk is fermented leafy greens, while achar adds a tangy, spicy, or savory accompaniment.

These foods reflect regional ingredients and food-preservation traditions. How they are prepared and served varies across Nepal.

02 / Street-side favorites

Street Food & Snacks

Spicy, savory, crunchy, and perfect for a snack break with friends or a walk through a busy market.

MomoSamosaPani PuriChatpatePakodaSekuwaChatamari

Momo

Steamed or fried dumplings, commonly served with a flavorful dipping sauce and enjoyed as a snack or meal.

Fillings and seasonings vary, and momos are often shared with friends or family. They are commonly served with a tomato-based or spicy dipping sauce.

Samosa & Pani Puri

Samosas are crisp pastries with savory fillings. Pani puri combines crisp shells, fillings, and tangy spiced water for a lively mix of flavors.

Both are popular snack choices, with crisp textures and bold flavors. Recipes and fillings vary by vendor and region.

Chatpate, Pakoda, Sekuwa & Chatamari

Chatpate is a popular spicy, tangy snack. Pakoda are battered and fried bites; sekuwa is grilled or skewered food; chatamari is a Newar rice-flour dish sometimes called Nepali pizza.

These foods show the range of Nepali snack traditions, from quick street-side mixes to grilled dishes and rice-flour specialties.

03 / Celebrations & sweet moments

Sweets & Festival Foods

Special foods connect festivals and family gatherings with traditions passed from one generation to the next.

Sel RotiYomariMalpuwaFestival Sweets

Sel Roti

A ring-shaped rice-flour bread with a lightly sweet taste, often prepared for celebrations and family gatherings.

Its crisp outside and softer centre make it a familiar festive treat. Families often prepare it at home for celebrations and visits.

Yomari

A steamed rice-flour delicacy associated with the Newar festival of Yomari Punhi, often filled with sweet ingredients such as chaku or sesame.

The sweet filling and shape are part of the dish's distinctive character. Yomari is especially associated with Newar food traditions and Yomari Punhi.

Malpuwa & Festival Sweets

Sweet foods and special recipes can be part of festive tables. What is prepared depends on regional traditions, family preferences, and the occasion.

Festival sweets differ by community, region, and occasion, and are often prepared or shared during family gatherings.

Sel roti is often associated with Dashain and Tihar, while yomari is connected with the Newar festival of Yomari Punhi.

04 / Slow down & sip

Chiya & Everyday Moments

A warm cup of tea can accompany a morning routine, a break at work, a visit with a neighbor, or an unhurried afternoon.

01 / A familiar cup

Dudh Chiya

Milk tea is a familiar comfort in many homes, shops, and tea stalls.

It may be prepared with tea, milk, sugar, and sometimes spices. The balance of ingredients depends on personal taste and household habits.

02 / A simple pause

Black & Spiced Tea

Tea can be served plain or brewed with spices for a warming, fragrant drink.

Preparation varies, and a cup of tea may mark a quiet break, a conversation with a friend, or a welcome for a guest.

03 / Tea-time together

Tea & Shared Snacks

Chiya is often enjoyed alongside snacks or during a visit with family, neighbors, or friends.

Offering tea is a simple gesture of hospitality. What is served with it can depend on the time of day, the household, and the occasion.

A place at the table

Food is a way of welcoming people.

Sharing a meal, offering a snack, or pouring a cup of tea can be a simple expression of care. Nepal's food traditions are diverse, and every region, community, and family adds its own flavor to the story.
