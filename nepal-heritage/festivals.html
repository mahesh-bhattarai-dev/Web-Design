Traditions, memories, and moments shared

More than a celebration

Every festival carries a story.

Festivals in Nepal can bring together family, neighbors, music, food, prayer, and long-standing customs. Some celebrate religious stories, some mark seasonal changes, and others create special moments for family and community.

Nepal is culturally diverse. The festivals people observe, and the ways they celebrate them, vary across communities, regions, and families.

Explore the traditions

Four celebrations to discover.

Start with these well-known festivals and discover the different meanings and customs associated with them.

01 · Family & blessings

Dashain

A time for family gatherings, blessings, and traditions passed between generations.

Dashain is an important festival for many Nepali Hindus. Families may gather, visit relatives, prepare special meals, and exchange good wishes. Vijaya Dashami is especially associated with receiving Tika and blessings from elders.

Tika, often prepared with red color, rice, and yogurt, and the green shoots known as jamara are familiar parts of many Dashain celebrations. Customs differ between families and communities.

02 · Light & connection

Tihar

Often known as a festival of lights, Tihar brings glowing lamps, colorful decorations, flowers, and family traditions to many homes.

The multi-day celebration includes observances associated with crows, dogs, cows, and other traditions. Laxmi Puja and Bhai Tika are also important parts of the festival for many families.

03 · Color & joy

Holi

Holi is known for vibrant colors, playful gatherings, and the arrival of spring. In many places, people celebrate by sharing colored powder and enjoying time with friends and family.

In Nepal, Holi celebrations take place on different dates in different regions. The atmosphere can range from neighborhood gatherings to large public celebrations.

04 · Devotion & gathering

Teej

Teej is observed by many Hindu women and is associated with devotion to Goddess Parvati and Lord Shiva. Celebrations can include prayer, songs, dancing, colorful clothing, and gatherings with relatives and friends.

Fasting practices and personal observances vary. The festival can also be an occasion for women to gather, celebrate, and share experiences with one another.

A closer look

Tika: A blessing shared across generations.

For many families, receiving Tika during Dashain is a meaningful moment. Elders may place Tika on younger relatives' foreheads and offer blessings for well-being, happiness, and a good future.

Jamara, family visits, and shared meals can make the occasion especially memorable. The exact practices and words of blessing vary by family and tradition.

One country, many traditions

Every family has its own story.

These festivals are only a starting point. Nepal's communities have many additional celebrations and regional customs, and even the same festival can be experienced differently from one family to another. Learning about these differences is part of appreciating the country's rich cultural heritage.
