The meaning behind the custom

Traditions are more than gestures.

A greeting, a blessing, a shared meal, or a family gathering can carry meaning beyond what we see. Nepali customs reflect many different histories, faiths, communities, and ways of life. Their meanings can also vary between families.

01 / Wishes for well-being

Tika & Blessings

Tika is a mark placed on the forehead in different Nepali customs. During Dashain, many families receive tika and blessings from elders and relatives. The occasion can express affection, good wishes, family connection, and hopes for well-being and prosperity.

The materials, ceremonies, and meanings vary by occasion, religious tradition, community, and family. Tika is not exclusive to Dashain; its use depends on the context.

02 / Everyday courtesy

Greetings & Respect

Namaste is a familiar greeting made with the palms joined together, often accompanied by a slight bow. Greetings in Nepal vary by language, region, relationship, and situation. A warm welcome and considerate behavior can be just as meaningful as a formal gesture.

Respect for elders is an important value in many families. It may be expressed through language, greetings, listening, offering help, or participating in family customs. People and generations may express respect in different ways.

03 / Connection & belonging

Family & Community

Family gatherings, helping relatives, celebrating milestones, and supporting neighbors are meaningful parts of life for many Nepali communities. Festivals, weddings, births, and other important events can bring people together across generations.

Family structures and expectations are not the same for everyone. Traditions continue to evolve as people balance inherited customs with their own choices and circumstances.

04 / Making people feel welcome

Hospitality & Kindness

Offering a guest tea, preparing food, sharing what is available, or making time for a conversation can be simple expressions of hospitality. The gesture does not need to be elaborate to make someone feel welcome.

Food and hospitality also connect this tradition to everyday Nepali life. The dishes served, the way guests are welcomed, and the expectations around visits vary across households and communities.
